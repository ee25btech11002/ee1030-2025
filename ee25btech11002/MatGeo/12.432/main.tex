\textbf{Question:}\\
If any two columns of 
$\Vec{\vec{p}} = \myvec{4 & 7 & 8 \\
                  3 & 1 & 5 \\
                  9 & 6 & 2 }$
are interchanged, which one of the following statements regarding the value of the determinant is CORRECT?
\begin{enumerate}
\item Absolute value remains unchanged but sign will change.  
\item Both absolute value and sign will change.  
\item Absolute value will change but sign will not change.  
\item Both absolute value and sign will remain unchanged.  
\end{enumerate}


\textbf{Solution:}\\
\begin{align}
    \Vec{{P}} =  \myvec{4 & 7 & 8 \\
                  3 & 1 & 5 \\
                  9 & 6 & 2 }
\end{align}
Interchanging columns 1 and 2 of $\vec{P}$ is equivalent to multiplying $\vec{P}$ on the right by the elementary matrix $\vec{E}$ that interchanges columns 1 and 2:
\begin{align}
\vec{E} = \myvec{
0 & 1 & 0\\
1 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 1
}
\end{align}

Hence, the new matrix is
\begin{align}
\vec{P'} = \vec{P}\vec{E}
\end{align}
We know that
\begin{align}
\det\brak{\vec{P'}} = \det\brak{\vec{P}\vec{E}} = \det\brak{\vec{P}} \cdot \det\brak{\vec{E}}
\end{align}

Now, the determinant of $\vec{E}$ is
\begin{align}
\det\brak{\vec{E}} 
= 
\myvec{
0 & 1 & 0\\
1 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 1
}
= -1
\end{align}

Hence,
\begin{align}
\det\brak{\vec{P'}} 
&= \det\brak{\vec{P}} \cdot \det\brak{\vec{E}} \\
&= \det\brak{\vec{P}} \cdot \brak{-1} \\
&= -\det\brak{\vec{P}}
\end{align}

Hence, on interchanging any two columns of a matrix:\\
The sign of the determinant changes, but the absolute value remains the same
